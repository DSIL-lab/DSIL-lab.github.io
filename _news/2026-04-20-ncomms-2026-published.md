- Title: Wafer-scale uniform non-ferroelectric κ-phase In₂Se₃ transistors ([link](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72553-y))

    
    This work proposes non-ferroelectric κ-phase indium selenide (In₂Se₃) as a logic transistor channel, resolving the hysteresis that has long limited In₂Se₃ devices while achieving both wafer-scale uniform growth and back-end-of-line (BEOL) process compatibility.

    Two-dimensional In₂Se₃ combines high carrier mobility with ferroelectricity, making it an attractive material for processing-in-memory applications. However, the α, β, and γ phases studied to date exhibit polarization-induced hysteresis, making them unsuitable for logic circuits that must return the same output for the same input. Neither CVD nor mechanical exfoliation could deliver uniform large-area growth while staying within the sub-450°C thermal budget.

    The proposed κ phase has a unit cell that stacks ferroelectric and dielectric regions in alternation, where the resulting depolarization field suppresses spontaneous polarization. Thermal evaporation followed by a 250°C anneal yielded a layered film approximately 9.9 nm thick, uniform across a full 4-inch wafer, with κ-phase formation and non-ferroelectric behavior confirmed by XRD, Raman, STEM, and PFM analyses. A 24×24 array of 576 devices showed uniform characteristics regardless of position, achieving an average field-effect mobility of 39.3 cm²V⁻¹s⁻¹ and an on/off current ratio spanning more than eight orders of magnitude.

    At the system level, monolithic integration with a p-type selenium-alloyed tellurium oxide (Se-alloyed TeOₓ) device produced a complementary inverter with full-swing operation and high voltage gain, demonstrating the potential of κ-In₂Se₃ as a scalable 2D semiconductor platform for BEOL-compatible logic integration.

    The work was carried out by Prof. Jimin Kwon's group at KAIST DSIL in collaboration with Prof. Yong-Young Noh's group in the Department of Chemical Engineering at POSTECH and Prof. Ao Liu's group at UESTC. Dr. Yongwoo Lee of our group served as co-first author alongside Jaeyun Lee of POSTECH.
