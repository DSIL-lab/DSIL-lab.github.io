## What you will do

You will:
- Extract physical metrics (e.g., number of I/Os, bump pitch, interconnect loss) from various packaging structures such as HBM, chiplets, and glass interposers, and translate them into model parameters for system-level evaluation.
- Utilize system/architecture simulators to run and model the performance of proposed hardware structures under real AI accelerator and LLM workload environments.
- Identify memory bandwidth bottlenecks occurring in 2.5D/3D integration and HBF (High Bandwidth Fan-out) environments, and comprehensively analyze how effectively new integration methods alleviate them.
- Evaluate the impact of power delivery, thermal paths, and interconnect constraints—essential considerations in 3D ICs and high-density integration systems—on overall system throughput and energy efficiency.
- Quantitatively verify and compare the system performance (e.g., system delay, total energy, bandwidth density) when applying new advanced devices and packaging technologies against existing standard architectures.
- Based on architecture-level performance prediction results, provide reverse feedback to colleagues researching devices and processes, proposing physical scaling directions and guidelines for optimal system configurations.
- Go beyond merely "functional" integration structures to proactively explore and prove optimal designs that maximize performance gains among various system architecture combinations, including 3D stacking.

## Who we are looking for

- Degree in Electrical Engineering, Computer Science, or a related interdisciplinary major.
- Completed coursework or possess a fundamental understanding of system architecture, computer architecture, or high-performance computing (HPC).
- Strong interest in understanding and modeling how individual device characteristics translate into overall system performance.
- Proficiency in or willingness to learn programming languages required for system simulation and modeling, such as C/C++, Python, and Verilog.
- Research experience using system simulators (e.g., gem5) or unafraid to learn and apply new simulation tools.
- Interest in understanding the operational principles of modern computing workloads (e.g., AI accelerators, LLMs) and quantitatively analyzing their bottlenecks.
- Excellent collaboration skills to smoothly communicate and exchange results with device and process researchers based on a system-level understanding.
- A proactive research attitude, eager to ask questions and engage in discussions with peers to materialize ideas.
- Availability for long-term research participation and a strong sense of responsibility regarding research schedules.

For non-Korean applicants, basic proficiency in Korean or a strong willingness to learn the language is highly preferred for effective communication within the team and with collaborators.
